## 📖 About the Publication

**Full title:** *Combined Noise Exposure and Hearing Difficulty as Independent Correlates of Tinnitus-Related Trouble Falling Asleep: A Nationally Representative Analysis of U.S. Adults*

**Journal:** *Noise & Health*, 2026, **28**(133), 985–992  
**Published online:** August 31, 2026  
**DOI:** [10.4103/nah.nah_81_26](https://doi.org/10.4103/nah.nah_81_26)

This peer-reviewed, cross-sectional study explores how **occupational and recreational noise exposure**, together with **self-reported hearing difficulty**, relate to tinnitus-associated difficulty falling asleep in U.S. adults. It uses data from the **2014 National Health Interview Survey (NHIS)**.

> **Repository purpose:** A publication showcase and citation resource. It does **not** host the analysis code, datasets, journal PDF, or reproductions of published figures. The original paper and its linked research code are accessible through the references above.

## 🔬 Study at a Glance

| Aspect | Published study |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Design** | Cross-sectional, nationally representative survey analysis |
| **Data source** | 2014 NHIS, including the hearing supplement |
| **Full survey sample** | 36,697 adult respondents |
| **Primary analytic sample** | 4,292 adults with tinnitus and eligible responses |
| **Exposure** | Occupational noise, leisure noise, both, or neither |
| **Primary outcome** | Tinnitus-related trouble falling asleep |
| **Statistical framework** | Survey-weighted logistic regression with design-based standard errors |
| **Additional analyses** | Sensitivity analyses, hearing protection, and exploratory decomposition |

## 📊 Selected Published Findings

- **41.8%** of adults in the tinnitus analytic sample reported tinnitus-related trouble falling asleep.
- Combined occupational and leisure noise exposure was associated with greater **adjusted odds** of this outcome relative to no major exposure: **aOR 1.69 (95% CI: 1.30–2.19)**.
- Self-reported hearing difficulty was also associated with increased adjusted odds: **aOR 1.78 (95% CI: 1.47–2.15)**.

These findings are **observational associations**, not estimates of causal effects. For full model specifications, confidence intervals, limitations, and interpretation, see the [published article](https://doi.org/10.4103/nah.nah_81_26).
